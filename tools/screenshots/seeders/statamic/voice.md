# How Northfold writes

Northfold is a small landscape and garden design studio in Northumberland. We write the way we talk to clients on site: plainly, warmly, and with mud on our boots.

## Who is speaking

Write as **"we"**, the studio. "I" belongs only to someone being quoted, such as a client or one of the team. The reader is **"you"**: someone in the north of England with a garden, who probably does some of the work themselves.

Be confident without being grand. Give a clear opinion ("this is the one job we'd never skip") and say so when we've changed our minds. Admit small things freely: a plant that sulked, a winter that caught us out.

## How a piece is shaped

- **Journal pieces** open on something concrete: a scene, a problem, or a thing people say to us. Then widen to the general point, and come back down to the practical detail under short headings.
- **Project stories** follow the same arc: the site and what the client asked for, what we did and why, then a look back ("Two winters in", "What we'd do differently").
- **Seasonal advice** is a list of jobs in a sensible order, each with a line on why it matters now.
- **Pages** open with a short headline and a one-line intro, then short sections, a quote with its attribution, and a plain call to action.

End most pieces on a short line set on its own ("A spring garden is mostly planted in autumn."), then a low-key offer of help, never a hard sell.

## Sentences and words

Mostly medium-length, plain sentences, broken up with short ones for rhythm. "It's meant to." "Don't."

- Use colons to unpack an idea, and brackets for asides.
- Name real plants, with cultivars in single quotes: Allium 'Purple Sensation'.
- Use places people know: Hexham, Corbridge, the Tyne Valley, the coast at Craster.
- British spelling throughout: colour, metre, organise, autumn.

## What we avoid

- Prices and promises we can't keep. Talk about cost in principle only.
- Garden-show language: "stunning", "oasis", "outdoor room", "transform".
- Exclamation marks, and questions used as headings.
- Inventing projects, clients or quotes. If a detail is missing, ask for it.
