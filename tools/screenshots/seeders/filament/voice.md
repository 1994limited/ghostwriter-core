# How Northfold writes

Northfold is a small landscape and garden design studio on Wharf Lane, in the North East. We write the way we talk to clients on a site walk: plainly, warmly, and as people who have their hands in the soil most weeks.

## Who we are writing for

- People with a garden they want to change, from a terraced backyard to a few acres in Northumberland.
- Schools, parents' associations and community groups with a space and a small budget.
- Other gardeners who like to know how and why, not just what.

Assume a curious reader who is not a professional. Explain a term the first time it appears, then use it.

## Tone

- **Plain and warm.** Short sentences, everyday words. "We dug the trench" rather than "the trench was excavated".
- **First person plural.** We are the studio: "we planted", "we would suggest".
- **Honest about trade-offs.** Say what something costs, what went wrong and what we would do differently.
- **Practical.** Every advice piece leaves the reader with something to do this week.
- **Never salesy.** No "transform your outdoor space", no exclamation marks, no superlatives we can't stand behind.

## Words we use, and words we avoid

| We say | We avoid |
|---|---|
| garden | outdoor space, outdoor room |
| planting | softscape |
| the people who use it | end users, stakeholders |
| it will settle in | it will thrive |
| site walk | consultation |

British spelling throughout: colour, metre, neighbour, organise.

## How a piece is shaped

1. **Open on the place or the season**, in one or two sentences: the slope, the shade, the month.
2. **Say what was wanted**, in the client's own terms where we can.
3. **Walk through what we did**, under short headings that are plain statements ("Paths before planting").
4. **End with what we learned**, or what to do next. Advice posts close with an offer of a site walk, said once and lightly.

Use lists for things a reader will do or check; use paragraphs for everything else.

## Details that make it ours

- Name plants by their common name first, with the botanical name only where it helps.
- Give real measurements and times: "five centimetres of compost", "three weeks".
- Credit the people who did the work, such as the waller or the nursery.
- Go back to a garden after a year or two and say honestly how it has turned out.
