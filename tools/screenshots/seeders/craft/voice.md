# How Northfold writes

## Who is talking, and to whom

We write as **"we"**, the studio: five people near Corbridge who design gardens and then come back to see how they grow. "Northfold" is for plain facts about the business ("Northfold is ten years old this month"). "I" only turns up inside a quote from a named person.

The reader is someone with a real patch of ground: a homeowner, a school, a community group. Call them **"you"** and assume they garden, or would like to. Write as if you were talking to them across a garden wall: friendly, practical, in no hurry.

## What it sounds like

- **Plain and warm.** Short sentences, everyday words, no jargon. If a technical term is needed, explain it the first time in a few words.
- **Practical.** Every piece leaves the reader knowing what to do, when, and roughly what it costs in time or money.
- **Honest.** Say what didn't work as readily as what did: "Not everything has thrived." Confidence about method, never about results we haven't seen.
- **Dry, light humour**, used sparingly and never at the reader's expense.
- **Specific.** Named places, plants and months, not "a variety of plants" or "the right time of year".

## How a piece is shaped

**Journal entries** open with a one-line standfirst, then a concrete scene or a plain claim. A short pivot line ("Here is why.") leads into the detail. Most carry one quote near the end, from a client or the studio. They close with a quiet invitation ("If you'd like help planning one, get in touch.") or a short, wry line.

**Pages** open with a one-line welcome and a button, run through short headed sections, include one testimonial signed with a first name and a place ("Ruth, Corbridge"), and end with a question-led prompt such as "Have a garden in mind?"

## Words and spelling

- British spelling and idiom: colour, fertiliser, fortnight, centimetres.
- Homely, physical words: potter, muddy knees, earn their keep, sulk.
- Botanical names in italics, cultivars in single quotes, common names in lower case: *Allium* 'Purple Sensation', tulips, yellow rattle.
- Measurements in digits with the unit spelled out: "about 15 centimetres deep".
- Prices plainly in pounds: "a first visit costs £240".

## Titles and headings

Sentence case, descriptive, unforced: "Making a meadow from a lawn", "Why we start every project with a soil pit". Headings inside a piece are short and plain: "What we did", "A year on", "Is it worth it?"

## Never

- Exclamation marks, "stunning", "transform", "solutions", "bespoke".
- Promises about how a garden will look in a year: plants have their own ideas.
- Client names or addresses that aren't already public.
